\section{Company Presentation}

\subsection{Company Overview}

ST2i (Soci\'et\'e Tunisienne d'Informatique pour l'Ing\'enierie) is a
Tunisian information technology company founded in 1992 and a subsidiary of
the STUDI Group, an international engineering group established in 1970
with operations across Africa and Europe.

The company specializes in Information Technology (IT), Information Systems
(IS), Geographic Information Systems (GIS), and Telecommunications. ST2i
provides end-to-end digital solutions, covering business analysis, software
development, system integration, deployment, maintenance, and technical
support.

With a multidisciplinary team of engineers, consultants, and technical
experts, ST2i delivers digital transformation projects for both public and
private organizations in Tunisia and internationally.

\subsection{Business Sector}

ST2i operates in the Information and Communication Technology (ICT) sector
as an IT services and consulting company. Its core activities include:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Information systems consulting and analysis
  \item Custom software development
  \item Software integration
  \item Geographic Information Systems (GIS)
  \item Digital transformation consulting
  \item Telecommunications solutions
  \item Technical support, maintenance, and user training
\end{itemize}

The company's solutions serve multiple industries, including public
administration, telecommunications, banking, healthcare, education, energy,
transportation, and infrastructure.

\subsection{Organization}

ST2i follows a collaborative organizational structure bringing together
consulting, functional, and technical teams. Its main departments include
Executive Management, Business Development, Project Management, Consulting
and Business Analysis, Software Development, Infrastructure and Systems
Integration, and Quality Assurance and Technical Support. Project teams
typically consist of a Project Manager, Business Analysts, Software
Engineers, QA Engineers, and Technical Experts.

\section{Existing Environment}

ST2i bids on public and international tenders (often funded by
institutions such as the World Bank or GIZ) that specify one or more
required expert positions. Matching internal experts to these positions is
currently done manually: a business analyst reads the Terms of Reference
(TDR) and searches the internal CV database by memory or keyword search,
with no systematic scoring, no anomaly checking, and no audit trail
explaining why a given expert was or was not selected.

\subsection{Identified Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Slow}: manually reading a TDR and cross-referencing dozens
    of CVs does not scale as the number of active tenders grows.
  \item \textbf{Inconsistent}: two analysts reviewing the same TDR may reach
    different conclusions, with no documented rationale to reconcile them.
  \item \textbf{Error-prone}: a missed qualified expert can mean passing on
    a winnable bid; a missed disqualifying gap (an expired certification, an
    unmet minimum-experience requirement) can mean committing to staff who
    do not actually meet a client's mandatory requirements.
  \item \textbf{No institutional memory}: past staffing decisions are not
    captured in a form that could calibrate future ones.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Statement}

Given a TDR describing one or more required expert positions and ST2i's
internal CV database, how can the company reliably determine, for each
position, which employees are qualified -- with enough explanation that a
human reviewer can trust and, when necessary, override the result?

\section{Proposed Solution}

AdéquIA automates this matching step end to end: it parses CVs and TDRs
into structured data, retrieves and scores candidates against each
required position using a combination of rule-based hard filters, weighted
embedding similarity, and LLM judgment for qualitative criteria, flags
anomalies (date inconsistencies, expired certifications, implausible
claims), and produces a citation-backed rationale for every score. A human
reviewer stays in the loop at every decision point: every automated
ranking can be approved, rejected, or overridden, and that decision is
written back to inform future scoring. The full technical design is
detailed in the project's architecture specification; the remainder of
this report focuses on how it was built, sprint by sprint.
