\section{Internship Context}

This report covers a one-month internship carried out at ST2i, a Tunisian IT
services and consulting company (a subsidiary of the STUDI Group), between
July 1 and July 31, 2026. The first five days (July 1--5) were spent on
Scrum setup and product-backlog writing (Chapter~\ref{chap:project-management}):
defining the roles, artifacts, and events to run the internship as a solo
Scrum cadence, and breaking the project down into the 33 stories and 170
points delivered across the four one-week sprints that follow, each ending
with a working, demoable increment.

\section{Problem Statement}

ST2i bids on public and international tenders that require staffing one or
more \textit{expert positions} defined in a Terms of Reference (TDR)
document: each position specifies a mandatory diploma, a minimum number of
years of experience, required certifications, languages, and sector
experience. Determining, for every required position, which employees are
actually qualified is today entirely manual: a business analyst reads the
TDR, often 100+ pages long, and tries to recall or search which experts
might fit, with the company even paying for a ChatGPT Plus subscription
just to upload and read a 200-page TDR, a cost for a workaround, not a
solution. Underneath sits the deeper problem: no analyst holds an accurate,
up-to-date picture of every employee's diplomas, certifications, and
project history in their head.

This does not fail gracefully as the company grows: the more positions and
employees to check, the more likely a qualified match or disqualifying gap
gets missed, either way at a direct cost (a winnable bid passed on, or
unqualified staff committed after a bid is won). A process whose
reliability erodes exactly as the company gets bigger caps how many
tenders ST2i can safely pursue, with real revenue on the other side of
every missed bid.

\section{Objectives}

The internship's objective was to design and build \textbf{AdéquIA}, a
decision-support system replacing this manual process with a repeatable one:
given a parsed TDR and the internal CV database, produce for each position a
ranked list of candidates with a compatibility score, a breakdown by
criterion, flagged anomalies, and a citation-backed rationale a reviewer can
approve, reject, or override in minutes instead of hours, regardless of a
tender's size or bench size. Given the one-month timeline, scope was kept to
the matching, scoring, anomaly-detection, and reporting pipeline plus the
reviewer-facing dashboard: tender discovery, bid submission logistics,
custom model fine-tuning, and dedicated GPU-serving infrastructure were all
out of scope. The system runs entirely on free-tier cloud LLM providers plus
a single local model as a fallback, the same setup planned for deployment.
